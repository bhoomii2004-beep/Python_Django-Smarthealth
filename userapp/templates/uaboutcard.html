RFID technology

RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) is a wireless, contactless identification technology that uses radio waves to automatically identify, track, and manage objects, people, or assets through RFID tags and readers. Each RFID tag contains a microchip and antenna that store unique data. When the tag comes within range of an RFID reader, the information is transmitted instantly to a connected system for real-time processing—without the need for physical contact or line-of-sight scanning.

RFID systems typically include three main components: the tag, the reader, and the backend software/database. Tags can be passive (powered by the reader’s signal), active (battery-powered with longer range), or semi-passive. Readers capture the tag data and send it to software that records, analyzes, and displays the information for users.
